AI automation

AI automation that takes routine work off your team.

AI automation uses software and AI models to carry out repetitive business work on its own, such as sorting incoming requests, updating records and drafting replies. A person still decides which steps get checked before anything goes out. We build it on the tools you already use.

01, What it is

Workflows that run themselves.

AI automation, also called AI workflow automation, is automation in which an AI model reads free text, such as an email, a form or an invoice, and picks the next step instead of following a fixed rule. The rest of the workflow runs as ordinary automation. A person approves the steps you choose.

Classic automation, often called business process automation, follows fixed rules: when this happens, do that. When a task needs more judgement and several steps, we build AI agents instead. Both are part of our AI agents and automations service.

Talk to us about this

The work it removes

Copying details between systems, sorting the inbox, chasing missing information, writing the same reply again and building the weekly report by hand.

What you get

A working workflow with one clear goal and a number we track from the day it goes live. You choose which steps a person approves. Your team can change the workflow without code, and it runs on accounts your company owns.

For example

Say supplier invoices arrive by email as PDFs. The workflow reads each one, pulls out the supplier, date and amount, and enters them in your accounting system. When something looks odd, it sends the invoice to a person with a note on what to check. Nobody types the routine ones.

From a real project

A hotel owner on the Danish coast ran two busy Outlook inboxes. We built an AI email agent in n8n and Microsoft 365 that sorted both inboxes and wrote reply drafts for her to approve. In one week it sorted 121 mails and found the 15 that needed a reply. She told us the drafts were not what she wanted, so we paused them and built what she asked for instead: one mail at 08:00 every morning with the conversations still waiting for her answer.

03, Which tasks

Which tasks suit automation?

Not everything needs AI. Some tasks are best done by a fixed rule, others need a model to read and judge, and some should stay with a person. This is how we sort them.

Task typeExampleFits
Moving data between systems with fixed fieldsAn order number is copied from the shop to the accounting system.Fixed automation
Sending a message at a set timeA confirmation email the day before a meeting.Fixed automation
Compiling figures for a regular reportLast week’s sales from two systems in one sheet every Monday.Fixed automation
Reading incoming email and sorting itQuestions, complaints and orders land in separate queues.Needs AI
Pulling details out of documentsSupplier, date and amount from an invoice PDF.Needs AI
Writing a first draft of a standard replyA draft answer to a customer enquiry, approved by a team member.Needs AI, with approval
Negotiating a price or handling an unhappy customerA call about a late delivery.Leave to a person
Decisions with legal or financial weightApproving a credit agreement or settling a dispute.Leave to a person

Many workflows mix the types: a fixed rule moves the data, an AI model reads the email and a person approves whatever looks odd.

Signs a process is ready to automate

  • It repeats often, and each time it follows the same steps.
  • Input arrives in a predictable way: the same inbox, the same form, the same file type.
  • Someone can write the steps down, including the ones that need judgement, and explain when to choose what.
  • You know what a correct result looks like, so it can be checked.
  • The systems involved can be connected, or the data can be taken out another way.
  • Mistakes are fixable, or a team member can see the result before it reaches a customer.
  • Someone on your team owns the process and can answer questions about it.
  • You can put a number on it today, such as time, cases or errors, so we can measure whether it improves.
How we work

A fixed scope and a clear goal.
