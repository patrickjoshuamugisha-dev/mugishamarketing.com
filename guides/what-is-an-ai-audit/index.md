# What is an AI audit?

Last updated: 21 November 2026 · Reviewed by [Patrick Mugisha](https://mugishamarketing.com/about/), founder

An AI audit is a structured review of how your business actually works, carried out to find which tasks AI can take over and which it should not. It ends in a ranked roadmap: what to build first, what to leave alone and what each item should change. The same word is sometimes used for testing an AI system itself.

## What an audit looks at

A good audit starts with the work, not with a tool. The reviewer follows real cases through the business and looks at five things.

- **Volume and rhythm.** What repeats, how often and how many hours it takes.

- **How information moves.** Where it enters, who retypes it and where it gets stuck between systems.

- **Rules or judgement.** Which steps follow a fixed rule and which need a person to weigh something up.

- **Conditions.** The data involved, the rules that apply to it and where it may be stored.

- **Ownership.** Who owns each task today, who will own the result and what number should improve.

## What you get at the end

The main deliverable is a ranked roadmap. Each item names the task, the reason it sits where it does, what it needs (access, data, a person with time), the number it should move and what could go wrong. Ranking is a judgement. In our view it should weigh hours saved, risk and effort together, and it should say plainly what is not worth building.

Sometimes the best item is not AI at all. Anthropic, in its guide to building agents, recommends “finding the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed”. An audit that sends every task to AI has not done its job.

## AI audit, AI strategy, AI workshop or readiness assessment

The four terms overlap and vendors use them loosely. The table is our reading of how they are usually meant, not an official definition.

| | What it is | What you get | When it fits

| AI audit | A review of real work and systems, case by case | A ranked roadmap with a number to move for each item | You have repeated tasks and want to know what to build first

| AI strategy | A direction for the whole business over a longer period | A document on goals, priorities and principles | Leadership must agree where AI belongs before any project

| AI workshop | A shared working session with your people | Common understanding and a list of ideas | The team needs a shared picture, or ideas must be collected

| AI readiness assessment | A check of data, systems, skills and governance | A score or gap list against a chosen yardstick | You suspect data or access may block you before you start

## How to prepare

You can make an audit faster and cheaper to run by arriving with a few things in hand:

- Name the person who knows how the work runs and can answer questions during the week.

- Pick two or three tasks that annoy people most, even if you are unsure AI fits.

- Gather examples of real inputs: a handful of recent emails, orders or forms, with personal details removed where you can.

- List the systems in use and who holds the logins.

- Write down one number you wish would improve, such as days to answer a customer.

- Decide who may say yes to a first build once the roadmap arrives.

## How to judge an audit report

These are our own rules of thumb, not a standard.

### Signs of a good report

- It names the cases it followed and shows the steps it saw.

- Every item has an owner, a number to move and a reason for its rank.

- It says what not to build and what to fix without AI.

- Assumptions and gaps are written down as assumptions and gaps.

- You could hand it to another supplier or your own team and they could act on it.

### Signs of a weak report

- It is a tool list with no connection to your tasks.

- Gains are given as bold figures with no way to check them.

- Everything it proposes happens to need one product or one supplier.

- It skips data, access and who is responsible when something goes wrong.

## If you already use AI

Some audits also look at AI systems you already run. The US standards institute NIST says it “has developed a framework to better manage risks to individuals, organizations, and society associated with artificial intelligence (AI)”, released on 26 January 2023 and “intended for voluntary use”. It is one yardstick an audit can use for risk. It is not a certificate, and we do not claim that following it makes a system compliant with any law.

## What to do afterwards

- Choose the first roadmap item that is small, frequent and has a named owner.

- Write down what a good result looks like and how you will measure it from the first day.

- Let the system suggest and a person decide for the first weeks.

- Compare its work with what the person would have done, then adjust.

- Return to the roadmap and decide on the next item only when the first one has shown its number.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an AI audit in simple terms?

It is a review of how your business works that ends in a ranked list of what AI could take over, what it should not and what to build first. It starts with your tasks and data, not with a product.

### How is an AI audit different from an AI strategy?

An audit is concrete: it follows real work and produces a ranked list of items to build. A strategy is broader and sets direction and principles. A strategy without an audit can stay abstract, and an audit without a strategy can miss the bigger picture.

### Is an AI audit the same as an AI readiness assessment?

Not quite. A readiness assessment checks whether your data, systems and skills are ready for AI, usually against a yardstick. An audit asks which work to hand over and in what order. The two can be done together.

### Who should take part in an AI audit?

The people who do the work, one person who can answer questions about systems and data, and a decision maker who can approve a first build. Without the people who do the work, the audit only sees the official process.

### What should an AI audit report contain?

The cases followed, a ranked list of items, the number each should move, what each needs and what could go wrong. It should also list what is not worth building and which assumptions it could not check.

### Do we have to build with the same company that audited?

No. A good roadmap works without its author. You can hand it to your own team or another supplier. If you choose us, we build the first item and follow the agreed number after launch.
