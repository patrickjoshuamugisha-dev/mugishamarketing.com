# An AI chatbot for your website that answers like your team.

An AI chatbot for your website answers visitors’ questions in plain language, using your own prices, policies and product information. It can also take down details, book a meeting and pass the conversation to a person when it should. We build it, connect it to your systems and keep improving it after launch.

01, What it is

## A chatbot that knows your business.

An AI chatbot for a website, or website chatbot, is a chat window that reads visitors’ questions as they are written and answers from the material you give it: your FAQ, prices, product pages and policies. If it does not know the answer, it says so and hands the conversation to your team.

Older chatbots follow a fixed menu: pick an option, get a canned reply. An AI chatbot reads the question as it is written and can answer wordings it has not seen before.

### The work it removes

The same customer service questions arriving by email, phone and chat: opening hours, delivery, what is included and how to book. It also takes the first round of back and forth with new leads, so your team starts the conversation further along. When the job goes beyond answering, the chatbot can be the front of an [AI agent](https://mugishamarketing.com/ai-agents/) that acts on the request.

### What you get

A chatbot on your website, built on your own information and set up to match your tone. It collects the details your team needs, books meetings in your calendar and hands over to a person with the whole conversation attached. You decide which topics it handles and which it never touches.

### For example

Say a company that installs heat pumps gets the same questions about timing, how long an installation takes and what a visit involves. The chatbot answers from the company’s own material. A visitor who wants a quote leaves an address and a phone number, and the chatbot books a call. The salesperson can read the conversation before dialling.

03, Before launch

## What the chatbot needs from you.

How a chatbot differs from plain automation and from an AI agent is set out in the comparison table on our [AI agents](https://mugishamarketing.com/ai-agents/#what) page. Below is what the chatbot needs from you before it goes live.

### What we need from you before the chatbot goes live

- Your FAQ, prices, delivery terms and product information as text we may use.

- A decision on which topics it may handle and which it must never touch, such as complaints or legal questions.

- Hand-over rules: when it passes a conversation to a person, to whom and on which channel.

- Your opening hours and what happens outside them, for example the chatbot takes the enquiry and your team answers next morning.

- The languages it should answer in.

- Access to your calendar, CRM or helpdesk, if it should book meetings or open cases.

- A short notice for visitors that they are writing with an AI and how the conversation is stored, agreed with your privacy policy.

- A person who can update the answers when prices or terms change.

- The number it should move, such as questions answered without your team or meetings booked.

### What is an AI chatbot for a website?

It is a chat window on your site that understands questions written in everyday language and answers them from material you provide, such as your FAQ, prices and policies. Unlike an older menu-based chatbot, visitors do not have to pick from fixed options.

### How is it different from a general AI tool such as GPT?

A general tool answers from broad knowledge. Your chatbot answers from your own information and stays within the topics you choose. If it does not know, it says so and passes the question to your team instead of guessing.

### Can it hand a conversation over to a person?

Yes. You decide when it should, for example for complaints, quotes or anything it cannot answer. The person gets the details and the whole conversation, by email or inside your CRM or helpdesk.

### Is our customer data safe?

Visitors may type personal details into a chat, so we settle the rules first. We agree which data the chatbot may use and where conversations are stored, with GDPR and EU hosting as requirements where they apply. The chatbot runs on accounts your business owns.
